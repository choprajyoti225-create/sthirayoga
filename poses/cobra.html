Bhujangasana

Cobra

bhujanga — serpent

A gentle backbend that counters hours of sitting. It is powered by the back muscles, not by pushing with the hands.

B
Hold3–5 breaths

How to do it

  1. 1

    Lie on your belly with legs extended and the tops of the feet on the mat.

  2. 2

    Place the hands under the shoulders and hug the elbows in.

  3. 3

    Press the pubic bone down and lengthen the tailbone toward the heels.

  4. 4

    On an inhale, peel the chest forward and up, using the back more than the arms.

  5. 5

    Keep the neck long and the shoulders drawn down the back.

Focus:Upper backChestSpine

Why practise it

  • Strengthens the muscles along the spine
  • Opens the chest and front of the shoulders
  • Counteracts slouched posture

Make it gentler

Lift the hands an inch off the mat for a "baby cobra" that relies only on back strength.

Take care

Avoid with recent back injury or in later pregnancy; keep the lift low if the lower back pinches.
