What yoga actually is

Poses are one part of eight.

More than 1,600 years ago the sage Patanjali compiled the Yoga Sutras, 196 short threads describing a path with eight “limbs”. The postures most people know as yoga are the third. The rest describe how to live, breathe and pay attention.

You don’t need to adopt any of it to enjoy a class — but knowing the map makes the practice richer.

  1. 01

    Yama

    Restraints

    How we treat others: non-harming, truthfulness, not stealing, moderation, non-grasping.

  2. 02

    Niyama

    Observances

    How we treat ourselves: cleanliness, contentment, discipline, self-study, surrender.

  3. 03

    Asana

    Posturethe poses

    Originally a steady, comfortable seat for meditation. Today, the physical poses.

  4. 04

    Pranayama

    Breath

    Extending and directing the breath to influence energy and attention.

  5. 05

    Pratyahara

    Withdrawal

    Drawing the senses inward so outside noise has less pull.

  6. 06

    Dharana

    Concentration

    Holding attention on a single point — the breath, a sound, an image.

  7. 07

    Dhyana

    Meditation

    Concentration that becomes effortless and continuous.

  8. 08

    Samadhi

    Absorption

    A state where the sense of separation between observer and observed dissolves.

Your first class

Questions everyone is too shy to ask.

Do I need to be flexible?

No. Saying you are too stiff for yoga is like saying you are too dirty for a shower. Flexibility is a by-product, not an entry requirement.

What should I wear and bring?

Clothes you can move in that stay put when you bend over. Bring water. Most studios rent mats, and practising barefoot is the norm.

Should I eat beforehand?

Aim for a light snack an hour or two before. Twisting and folding on a full stomach is rarely pleasant.

What if I can’t do a pose?

Take a variation, use a prop, or rest in Child’s Pose. A good teacher expects this and will never mind.

How often should I practise?

Two or three times a week is plenty to notice change within a month. Ten minutes at home counts.

Is yoga safe with an injury or condition?

Often yes, with adjustments. Tell your teacher before class, and check with your doctor if you are pregnant, recovering from surgery or managing a chronic condition.
