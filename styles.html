Styles of yoga

Same roots, very different rooms.

A Yin class and an Ashtanga class can feel like two separate activities. Here is what to expect from the five you’re most likely to see on a studio timetable.

What do you want from a class?

Choose one and we’ll point you to a style.

01

Hatha

The foundation everything else is built on.

In modern studios, “Hatha” usually means a slower class where each pose is held for several breaths with clear instruction. It is the ideal place to learn names, shapes and how to breathe while moving.

Pace

Heat

Typical class

60–75 min

  • Poses held for 3–6 breaths
  • Plenty of verbal cues
  • Breathwork and a short relaxation

Best for: First-timers and anyone who wants to learn alignment properly.

02

Vinyasa

Movement linked to breath, like a slow dance.

Vinyasa — “to place in a special way” — strings poses together so that each movement rides an inhale or exhale. No two classes are the same, and the pace can range from mellow to athletic.

Pace

Heat

Typical class

45–75 min

  • Sun salutations
  • Creative, flowing sequences
  • A cardio-style warmth by the end

Best for: People who like variety, music and a bit of a workout.

03

Yin

Long, quiet holds for the deep tissues.

Yin poses are mostly seated or lying down and held for three to five minutes with the muscles relaxed. The long holds gently load connective tissue and give the mind room to settle — or to notice how restless it is.

Pace

Heat

Typical class

60–90 min

  • Few poses, long holds
  • Props like bolsters and blocks
  • Lots of silence

Best for: Busy minds, stiff hips, athletes on recovery days.

04

Ashtanga

A fixed sequence, practised with discipline.

Popularised by K. Pattabhi Jois in Mysore, Ashtanga follows the same series of poses in the same order every time, linked with Ujjayi breath. Many students practise “Mysore style”: self-paced, with a teacher offering individual adjustments.

Pace

Heat

Typical class

75–105 min

  • Identical sequence each class
  • Strong, steady breath
  • Six days a week for devotees

Best for: Those who love structure, routine and measurable progress.

05

Restorative

Fully supported rest. Effort is optional.

Every pose is propped so completely that the body can let go. A class might include only five or six poses, each held up to ten minutes, often with blankets, eye pillows and dimmed lights.

Pace

Heat

Typical class

60–75 min

  • Heavy use of props
  • Very long, comfortable holds
  • Deep relaxation

Best for: Stress, poor sleep, illness recovery, and gentle beginnings.

Also on the timetable

Iyengar
Precise alignment, long holds and inventive use of props such as ropes and chairs.
Kundalini
Repetitive movement, breathwork, chanting and meditation in set “kriyas”.
Hot yoga
Classes in a heated room, often 35–40°C. Hydrate well and pace yourself.
Prenatal
Adapted for pregnancy, with focus on comfort, breath and pelvic floor awareness.
Chair yoga
All poses done seated on or supported by a chair. Great for limited mobility.
